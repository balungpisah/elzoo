## Outcome and boundary

The saga of settled decision 1, in one Assembly with its own durable log (Q10 A: no `workflow/saga` Piece). A **checkout** pays one invoice or tops up one wallet through steps that each belong to a ready Piece: the charge (`domain/payment-lifecycle`), the invoice payment (`domain/invoice`), and the journal posts (`domain/journal`), plus an optional host allowance step for a top-up. Each step is recorded before it starts and is idempotent by its key; a succeeded step's compensation is written in the same checkout transaction as its recorded result. After a step fails **definitely**, the checkout runs the compensations of the succeeded steps in reverse order, in parallel only where declared, each idempotent with its own key; a failed compensation is its own recorded outcome and stops the checkout for an operator. A step whose outcome is **unknown** registers nothing and is never compensated blindly: the checkout waits at it until the owning Piece reports a definite state.

No payment provider is named (Q9 D): the charge's provider is payment-lifecycle's provider Need, which the host gives, and the tests script it. The Assembly drives no intent itself: payment-lifecycle returns an intent admission, and the host starts and advances the `delivery/effect-intent` owner and calls `record/3`, as payment-lifecycle's README says; the checkout reads the payment's state.

Durable state, money, unknown outcomes and concurrency: this task keeps the local small gate and gets the full review of the coordinator guide ("Reviewer by risk").

## Interface

`Elzoo.LedgerCheckout` (`lib/elzoo/ledger_checkout.ex`) over plain Ecto/Postgrex tables on a consumer Repo.

- `Elzoo.LedgerCheckout.Migration.install(prefix)` installs `elzoo_checkout_checkouts` (`PRIMARY KEY (tenant, checkout_id)`: kind, the canonical declaration, state, failure reason, revision) and `elzoo_checkout_steps` (`PRIMARY KEY (tenant, checkout_id, step)`: position, group, state, recorded result, compensation, revision) under the tenant floor, and the README gives the minimum grants (no `DELETE`).
- `new(opts)` takes exactly `repo`, `prefix`, `payments` (an `%Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle{}`), `invoices` (an `%Elzoo.Invoice{}`), `journal` (an `%Elzoo.Journal{}`) and optionally `allowance`, `%{grant, revoke}` of one-arity host Needs. It returns `{:ok, checkout_binding}` or `{:error, :invalid}`.
- `open(b, tenant, request)` stores a checkout `:running`, idempotent by `checkout_id` (an equal request `{:ok, :duplicate}`, another `{:error, :conflict}`). `checkout_id` is 1..64 bytes of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`; `actor_ref` is a journal text. Either
  - `%{checkout_id, kind: :invoice_payment, invoice_id, amount, recipient, approved: %{amount, recipient}, approval_ref, settle: %{to, from}, actor_ref}`: steps `charge`, `invoice`, `settle`; or
  - `%{checkout_id, kind: :top_up, amount, recipient, approved, approval_ref, wallet: %{to, from}, allowance: nil | %{grant_id}, parallel: boolean, actor_ref}`: steps `charge`, `wallet` and, when `allowance` is set, `allowance`; `parallel: true` (only with an allowance) declares `wallet` and `allowance` one parallel group. A top-up with an allowance and no `allowance` Needs in the binding is `:invalid`.
- `resume(b, tenant, %{checkout_id})` runs the checkout as far as it can now and returns `{:ok, %{state, wait}}`: `state` is `:running | :compensating | :completed | :compensated | :needs_operator`, and `wait` is `nil` or `%{step, for: :charge_intent | :refund_intent | :retry, admission: admission | nil}`. Errors are fixed atoms (`:invalid`, `:in_transaction`, `:isolation`, `:unknown_checkout`, `:unavailable`) that never echo an ID or amount.
- `get(b, tenant, %{checkout_id})` returns the checkout with its steps and their compensations; `list_running(b, tenant, %{after: nil | checkout_id, limit: 1..256})` returns `{:ok, %{checkout_ids, more}}` of the checkouts in `:running` or `:compensating`, in byte order of `checkout_id`.

### Steps

Every call to another Piece or Need runs outside the checkout's own transactions (the Pieces refuse an open one); a checkout transaction only records. A step's row is written `started` before its first call. The step's result and its compensation are written together in one checkout transaction, once: a later recording of an equal result is a no-op, of another a `:conflict` that the README says cannot occur from the rules below.

- `charge`: `payment_id` is `"checkout." <> checkout_id`. If `get/3` finds no payment, `create/3` with the approved terms; a `:conflict` is a definite failure. Then by the payment's state: `:created`, `:pending` or `:unknown`, `charge/3` with the checkout's `amount` and `recipient`, and the checkout waits `:charge_intent` with the admission (the host drives the intent and calls `record/3`, then `resume/3` again); `charge/3`'s `:approval_mismatch` or `:currency` is a definite failure; `:succeeded` records the step with the compensation that `get/3` returns; `:failed` is a definite failure. A `:pending` or `:unknown` payment is never compensated: the checkout waits until the host's reconciliation (a verified event or `poll`) makes it definite.
- `invoice`: `apply_payment/3` with `payment_ref` `"checkout/<id>"` and the amount; `{:ok, _}` records it with the compensation `%{kind: :reverse_payment, reversal_id: "checkout/<id>/c/invoice", payment_ref, amount: :remaining}`.
- `settle` and `wallet`: `post/3` with `operation_id` `"checkout/<id>/settle"` or `"checkout/<id>/wallet"`, postings `to` +amount and `from` −amount; `{:ok, _}` records it with the compensation `%{kind: :reverse, operation_id: "checkout/<id>/c/<step>", reverses: <the step's operation_id>}`. After a crash between the journal's commit and the record, the next `resume/3` asks again by the same key, gets `:duplicate` and records the result and compensation then.
- `allowance`: the `grant` Need given `%{tenant, checkout_id, grant_id, amount}`, which the host makes idempotent by `grant_id`; `:ok` records it with the compensation `%{kind: :revoke, grant_id}`, `{:error, :refused}` is a definite failure.
- For every step and compensation: `{:error, :unavailable}` of a Piece, and any other answer, raise, throw or exit of a Need, makes the checkout wait `:retry` at that step with nothing recorded; another error of a Piece is a definite failure, recorded with its fixed reason.

### Compensation

After a definite failure the checkout is `:compensating`. It runs the recorded compensations of the succeeded steps in reverse order of their position; the members of a declared parallel group come before the steps ahead of the group and have no order between them (the README says whether they run concurrently or one after the other). The last step's compensation therefore runs only when a sibling of its parallel group fails.

- `:refund`: `refund/3` with `refund_id` `comp/charge` and `:remaining`, waiting `:refund_intent` with its admission until `get/3` shows that refund `:succeeded` (compensated) or `:failed` (failed); `:nothing_to_refund` is compensated (nothing is left to take back).
- `:reverse_payment`: `reverse_payment/3` as recorded; `{:ok, _}` and `:nothing_applied` are compensated.
- `:reverse`: `reverse/3` with `actor_ref` the checkout's; `{:ok, _}` and `:already_reversed` are compensated; `:insufficient_funds` (a spent wallet) and other refusals are failed.
- `:revoke`: the `revoke` Need given `%{tenant, checkout_id, grant_id}`; `:ok` is compensated, `{:error, :refused}` failed.

A failed compensation is recorded as that step's `compensation_failed` with its reason, no further compensation runs, and the checkout is `:needs_operator`; the compensations not run stay recorded. When every compensation is done the checkout is `:compensated` (also when the failed step was the first, with none). All steps succeeded makes it `:completed`.

`piece.json` declares the dependencies `domain/payment-lifecycle`, `domain/invoice`, `domain/journal`, `domain/money`, `state/tenant-isolation` and, for its tests, `delivery/effect-intent`; telemetry `not_applicable`, the config of `new/1`, control `not_applicable`; and the claims. The README states the host's side (driving the charge and refund intents, calling `resume/3` after each, finding running checkouts with `list_running/3`, the Needs' idempotency) and the Repo `:timeout` rule: the checkout's own transactions call no Need, so they are short; the reused Pieces' rules are theirs.

## Acceptance

On real disposable PostgreSQL 16 as the `elzoo_app` role (the helper fails, not skips, under the conditions above), with the ready Pieces, a scripted payment provider Need and the host's intent driving as payment-lifecycle's tests do. RC-41's list:

1. An invoice payment completes: one charge, one application (the invoice `:paid`), one settle operation, each step recorded with its compensation; balances are as posted.
2. A definite failure at each step compensates the earlier ones in reverse order exactly once: the charge declined (nothing to compensate, `:compensated`); the invoice refused (an invoice already paid by another payment, `{:state, :paid}`; the charge refunded); the settle refused (an unknown account; the application reversed, then the charge refunded, in that order, by the recorded order of calls); running `resume/3` again after `:compensated` changes nothing.
3. An unknown settlement of the charge: the provider times out, the checkout waits `:charge_intent` with nothing registered and nothing compensated; after a `poll` that reports success it continues to `:completed`; after one that reports failure it is `:compensated` with no refund.
4. Duplicate and out-of-order provider events applied by the host change nothing in the checkout's result.
5. An approval drift: `amount` other than the approved amount is `:approval_mismatch`, a definite failure before any intent.
6. A concurrent refund during a checkout: a host refund of part of the payment under another `refund_id` before the compensation; the `comp/charge` refund takes only the rest, and the payment is never over-refunded.
7. Balanced corrections: after a compensated top-up and a compensated invoice payment, every journal account's balance is back to its value before the checkout, and each reversal is linked to its operation.
8. Crash and resume: the state after a crash is made by performing a step's effect directly with the checkout's key (as a crashed run did) before `resume/3`, or by killing the resuming process (the task says which per case): after the journal post and before its record, `resume/3` records one post (`:duplicate`) and one compensation; after each other step likewise; `list_running/3` returns the checkout before the resume and not after it completes; `limit` 1 and 256, 257 refused, `more` at the edge.
9. Two concurrent `resume/3` calls behind a barrier: one journal operation, one application, one charge intent key, one recorded result and compensation per step.
10. A failed compensation: a top-up whose wallet credit was spent (the reversal `:insufficient_funds`) after a later allowance failure is `:needs_operator`, the wallet step `compensation_failed`, and the charge's refund not run and still recorded.
11. A declared parallel group: a top-up with `parallel: true` whose wallet post fails after the allowance succeeded revokes the allowance once, then refunds the charge; without `parallel`, an allowance failure reverses the wallet post, then refunds.
12. Cross-tenant isolation as `elzoo_app`: owner-only DDL refused, another tenant's checkout invisible to `get/3`, `resume/3` and `list_running/3`, a direct write of another tenant's row refused with SQLSTATE 42501.
13. Negative controls, one at a time, each restored before the final gates: compensate in forward order (case 2's order case fails); record the compensation in a separate transaction after the result, with a crash between (case 8 leaves a step without one); compensate a `:pending` charge (case 3 fails).

## Limits to state

The payment provider is scripted (Q9 D): no claim about a provider's settlement, idempotency or events; a balanced journal does not prove external settlement. The host drives the charge and refund intents and the allowance Needs, and calls `resume/3`; a checkout it never resumes stays running and is listed. A failed compensation stops the checkout for an operator, and no operator API is supplied. No inventory, per-step timeout or tax.

## Outcome and boundary

The saga of settled decision 1, in one Assembly with its own durable log (Q10 A: no `workflow/saga` Piece). A **checkout** pays one invoice or tops up one wallet through steps that each belong to a ready Piece: the charge (`domain/payment-lifecycle`), the invoice payment (`domain/invoice`), and the journal posts (`domain/journal`), plus an optional host allowance step for a top-up. Each step is recorded before it starts and is idempotent by its key; a succeeded step's compensation is written in the same checkout transaction as its recorded result. After a step fails **definitely**, the checkout runs the compensations of the succeeded steps in reverse order, in parallel only where declared, each idempotent with its own key; a failed compensation is its own recorded outcome and stops the checkout for an operator. A step whose outcome is **unknown** registers nothing and is never compensated blindly: the checkout waits at it until the owning Piece reports a definite state.

No payment provider is named (Q9 D): the charge's provider is payment-lifecycle's provider Need, which the host gives, and the tests script it. The Assembly drives no intent itself: payment-lifecycle returns an intent admission, and the host starts and advances the `delivery/effect-intent` owner and calls `record/3`, as payment-lifecycle's README says; the checkout reads the payment's state.

Durable state, money, unknown outcomes and concurrency: this task keeps the local small gate and gets the full review of the coordinator guide ("Reviewer by risk").

## Interface

`Elzoo.LedgerCheckout` (`lib/elzoo/ledger_checkout.ex`) over plain Ecto/Postgrex tables on a consumer Repo.

- `Elzoo.LedgerCheckout.Migration.install(prefix)` installs `elzoo_checkout_checkouts` (`PRIMARY KEY (tenant, checkout_id)`: kind, the canonical declaration, state, failure reason, revision) and `elzoo_checkout_steps` (`PRIMARY KEY (tenant, checkout_id, step)`: position, group, state, recorded result, compensation, revision) under the tenant floor, and the README gives the minimum grants (no `DELETE`).
- `new(opts)` takes exactly `repo`, `prefix`, `payments` (an `%Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle{}`), `invoices` (an `%Elzoo.Invoice{}`), `journal` (an `%Elzoo.Journal{}`) and optionally `allowance`, `%{grant, revoke}` of one-arity host Needs. It returns `{:ok, checkout_binding}` or `{:error, :invalid}`.
- `open(b, tenant, request)` stores a checkout `:running`, idempotent by `checkout_id` (an equal request `{:ok, :duplicate}`, another `{:error, :conflict}`). `checkout_id` is 1..64 bytes of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`. `open/3` checks every field against the bound of the Piece that takes it before it stores anything, so a shape fault costs no charge: `amount` and the approved terms by `domain/money` and payment-lifecycle's rules, `recipient` and `approval_ref` by payment-lifecycle's, `invoice_id` by invoice's, each account ID and `actor_ref` by the journal's (1..128 bytes of printable UTF-8), and `grant_id` 1..128 bytes of printable UTF-8; a fault is `{:error, :invalid}`. Either
  - `%{checkout_id, kind: :invoice_payment, invoice_id, amount, recipient, approved: %{amount, recipient}, approval_ref, settle: %{to, from}, actor_ref}`: steps `charge`, `invoice`, `settle`; or
  - `%{checkout_id, kind: :top_up, amount, recipient, approved, approval_ref, wallet: %{to, from}, allowance: nil | %{grant_id}, parallel: boolean, actor_ref}`: steps `charge`, `wallet` and, when `allowance` is set, `allowance`; `parallel: true` (only with an allowance) declares `wallet` and `allowance` one parallel group. A top-up with an allowance and no `allowance` Needs in the binding is `:invalid` at `open/3`, and `resume/3` of such a checkout through a binding without them is `{:error, :invalid}` with nothing called.
- `resume(b, tenant, %{checkout_id})` runs the checkout as far as it can now and returns `{:ok, %{state, wait}}`: `state` is `:running | :compensating | :completed | :compensated | :needs_operator`, and `wait` is `nil` or `%{step, for: :charge_intent | :refund_intent | :retry | :busy, admission: admission | nil}`. Errors are fixed atoms (`:invalid`, `:in_transaction`, `:isolation`, `:unknown_checkout`, `:unavailable`, `:conflict`) that never echo an ID or amount.
- **One holder per checkout.** `resume/3` runs inside `repo.checkout(fun, timeout: :infinity)` on the checkout's Repo (DBConnection's checkout deadline covers the whole checked-out time, 15,000 ms by default, `deps/db_connection/lib/db_connection/holder.ex` lines 318 and 442–456, and ending it closes the session; `integration/search-data` sets its own checkout `timeout:` for the same reason, `search_data.ex` lines 576–584), and first takes a session advisory lock with `pg_try_advisory_lock` on a 64-bit key derived from the prefix, tenant and `checkout_id` (a collision only makes two checkouts wait for each other). It holds the lock for the whole call, every call to another Piece or Need included, and releases it before it returns. A caller that does not get the lock calls no Piece and no Need and returns the stored state with `wait: %{step, for: :busy, admission: nil}`. So two concurrent `resume/3` calls never both call another Piece for one checkout, and a refusal that depends on another's state (an invoice paid by another payment, a journal account opened or funded later) is read by one holder only.
- `get(b, tenant, %{checkout_id})` returns the checkout with its steps and their compensations; `list_running(b, tenant, %{after: nil | checkout_id, limit: 1..256, state: :open | :needs_operator})` returns `{:ok, %{checkout_ids, more}}` of the checkouts in `:running` or `:compensating` (`state: :open`) or in `:needs_operator`, in byte order of `checkout_id`.

### Steps

Every call to another Piece or Need runs outside the checkout's own transactions (the Pieces refuse an open one); a checkout transaction only records. A step's row is written `started` before its first call. The step's result and its compensation are written together in one checkout transaction, once: a later recording of an equal result is a no-op, of another `resume/3`'s `{:error, :conflict}` with nothing more done (a guard; under the lock no rule here makes two results for one step).

- `charge`: `payment_id` is `"checkout." <> checkout_id`. If `get/3` finds no payment, `create/3` with the approved terms; a `:conflict` is a definite failure. Then by the payment's state: `:created`, `:pending` or `:unknown`, `charge/3` with the checkout's `amount` and `recipient`, and the checkout waits `:charge_intent` with the admission (the host drives the intent and calls `record/3`, then `resume/3` again); `charge/3`'s `:approval_mismatch` is a definite failure; `:succeeded` records the step with the compensation that `get/3` returns; `:failed` is a definite failure. A `:pending` or `:unknown` payment is never compensated: the checkout waits until the host's reconciliation (a verified event or `poll`) makes it definite.
- `invoice`: `apply_payment/3` with `payment_ref` `"checkout/<id>"` and the amount; `{:ok, _}` records it with the compensation `%{kind: :reverse_payment, reversal_id: "checkout/<id>/c/invoice", payment_ref, amount: :remaining}`.
- `settle` and `wallet`: `post/3` with `operation_id` `"checkout/<id>/settle"` or `"checkout/<id>/wallet"`, the fixed `reason` `"elzoo.ledger-checkout settle"` or `"elzoo.ledger-checkout wallet"` (Contract constants, in the README, since the reason is in the journal's request digest), postings `to` +amount and `from` −amount; `{:ok, _}` records it with the compensation `%{kind: :reverse, operation_id: "checkout/<id>/c/<step>", reverses: <the step's operation_id>}`. After a crash between the journal's commit and the record, the next `resume/3` asks again by the same key, gets `:duplicate` and records the result and compensation then.
- `allowance`: the `grant` Need given `%{tenant, checkout_id, grant_id, amount}`, which the host makes idempotent by `grant_id`, a refusal included (a repeated `grant` after `{:error, :refused}` answers it again), and which grants nothing after a `revoke` of that `grant_id` (it answers `{:error, :refused}`); the `revoke` Need is idempotent by `grant_id` too (a repeat answers `:ok`); `:ok` records it with the compensation `%{kind: :revoke, grant_id}`, `{:error, :refused}` is a definite failure.
- For every step and compensation: `{:error, :unavailable}` of a Piece, and any other answer, raise, throw or exit of a Need, makes the checkout wait `:retry` at that step with nothing recorded. `{:error, :in_transaction}` and `{:error, :isolation}` of a Piece (it checks them before its idempotency check, so they can follow an `:unavailable` that did commit) record nothing and are returned as `resume/3`'s own error. Another error of a Piece is a definite failure, recorded with its fixed reason.
- **The parallel group.** With `parallel: true`, `wallet` and `allowance` are both started whatever the other returns (concurrently or one after the other; the README says which). The group is decided only when both are definite: a member waiting `:retry` keeps the checkout waiting at it, and its sibling is never skipped. When one member fails, the group's failure is that member's; when both fail, the recorded failure reason is the `wallet` step's and each member's own failure is recorded on its row.

### Compensation

After a definite failure the checkout is `:compensating`. It runs the recorded compensations of the succeeded steps in reverse order of their position. The one parallel group is the last two steps of a top-up, so a failure inside it leaves at most one succeeded member, whose compensation runs first; no group ever has two compensations to run. The last step's compensation therefore runs only when a sibling of its parallel group fails.

- `:refund`: `refund/3` with `refund_id` `comp/charge` and `:remaining`, waiting `:refund_intent` with its admission until `get/3` shows that refund `:succeeded` (compensated) or `:failed` (failed); `:nothing_to_refund` is compensated (nothing is left to take back). `:remaining` leaves out what other refunds hold, a host refund still `:pending` or `:unknown` included, so the README says that `:compensated` covers the checkout's own refund only.
- `:reverse_payment`: `reverse_payment/3` as recorded; `{:ok, _}` and `:nothing_applied` are compensated.
- `:reverse`: `reverse/3` with `actor_ref` the checkout's and the fixed `reason` `"elzoo.ledger-checkout compensate"`; `{:ok, _}` and `:already_reversed` are compensated; `:insufficient_funds` (a spent wallet) and other refusals are failed.
- `:revoke`: the `revoke` Need given `%{tenant, checkout_id, grant_id}`; `:ok` is compensated, `{:error, :refused}` failed.

A failed compensation is recorded as that step's `compensation_failed` with its reason, no further compensation runs, and the checkout is `:needs_operator`; the compensations not run stay recorded. When every compensation is done the checkout is `:compensated` (also when the failed step was the first, with none). All steps succeeded makes it `:completed`.

`piece.json` declares the dependencies `domain/payment-lifecycle`, `domain/invoice`, `domain/journal`, `domain/money`, `state/tenant-isolation` and, for its tests, `delivery/effect-intent`; telemetry `not_applicable`, the config of `new/1`, control `not_applicable`; and the claims. The README states the host's side (driving the charge and refund intents, calling `resume/3` after each, finding running checkouts with `list_running/3`, the Needs' idempotency, the lock and its `:busy` wait) and the Repo `:timeout` rule: the checkout's own transactions call no Need, so they are short; the reused Pieces' rules are theirs. The lock holds one pool connection for the whole `resume/3`, Need and Piece calls included, with no checkout deadline (`timeout: :infinity`). With no deadline, no query on that connection has a client deadline either (a call on a checked-out connection starts none), the checkout's own transactions and those of a reused Piece bound to the same Repo included; so the host sets `statement_timeout` and `lock_timeout` for the role of the checkout's Repo, and bounds its own Needs. A Piece bound to the checkout's Repo runs on the checked-out connection; only a Piece bound to another Repo, or a group member run in another process, takes one more connection, and the pool size must allow for them.

## Limits to state

The payment provider is scripted (Q9 D): no claim about a provider's settlement, idempotency or events; a balanced journal does not prove external settlement. The host drives the charge and refund intents and the allowance Needs, and calls `resume/3`; a checkout it never resumes stays running and is listed, and a charge or refund intent that the host never brings to a definite state leaves the checkout listed for ever. The lock is a session lock on the checkout's Repo, so it needs a connection that keeps its session: a transaction-mode pooler in front of that Repo breaks it. If that connection is lost while a call to a Need or to a Piece bound to another Repo is in flight, the lock is released and a second `resume/3` can run beside the first; the first caller then cannot record (it returns `{:error, :unavailable}`), the effect of its call in flight stays unrecorded by it, and a refusal that the second holder reads may disagree with that effect. The README states this; it is not fenced. A query that waits without end (no `statement_timeout` or `lock_timeout` on the role) holds the lock, and every other `resume/3` of that checkout answers `:busy`. `:compensated` covers the checkout's own refund, not a host refund still pending. A failed compensation stops the checkout for an operator, and no operator API is supplied. No inventory, per-step timeout or tax.

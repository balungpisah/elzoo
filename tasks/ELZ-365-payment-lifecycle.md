## Outcome and boundary

One durable, provider-neutral Piece in plain Elixir, Ecto and Postgrex over a consumer-owned Ecto Repo on PostgreSQL 16: one **payment**'s charge and its **refunds**, each sent to the payment provider through a host **provider Need** as one `delivery/effect-intent` intent whose provider-side idempotency key derives from the logical payment or refund ID, never from an Attempt (the ELZ-025 rule of the `integration/payments` row); provider **events** checked by the provider Need and applied only forward; and, for a charge that succeeded, the **compensation** that undoes it. The Contract source is the `domain/payment-lifecycle` section of [the Wave 9 plan](../docs/plans/bot-platform-wave9.md) ("Level 1") and RC-24, under owner answers Q9 D and Q10 A (2026-11-24; "Owner answers (2026-11-24)" there) and settled decision 1 (the saga rules) with the plan's reading of it (one saga log, in `assembly/ledger-checkout`).

Under Q9 D no provider is named: this Piece names none, and `integration/payments` waits. The provider Need is a behaviour that the later provider Piece implements; this task's tests use a scripted provider. Under Q10 A and the reading of decision 1, this Piece keeps no saga log: the result of the step that makes a charge succeed holds its compensation, which the caller registers.

This task carries the PR #853 review answer on reversal after a partial refund into this Piece: a compensation refunds **what is still refundable when it runs**, not the charged amount, so a compensation after a partial refund is not refused as an over-refund.

The Piece keeps durable state, serializes concurrent refunds, takes untrusted provider events and has unknown outcomes, so this task keeps the local small gate and gets the full review of the coordinator guide ("Reviewer by risk"; `AGENTS.md` sections 5 and 6).

## Interface

Module `Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle`. Every function after `new/1` takes the binding, the tenant and one request map.

- `Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle.Migration.install(prefix)` installs the tenant-isolation readers and four protected tables: payments, `PRIMARY KEY (tenant, payment_id)`; refunds, `PRIMARY KEY (tenant, payment_id, refund_id)`; events, `PRIMARY KEY (tenant, event_id)`; observations, `PRIMARY KEY (tenant, payment_id, operation_ref, seq)`, one row for each provider answer that the Adapter received, send or poll, in the order received (`seq` from 1 per operation), with the intent's attempt number (`request.attempt`, which a poll of an attempt shares with its send), its `source` (`send` or `poll`) and an `applied` flag. No row is replaced. The rows of an operation are bounded by the host's poll count; the README states it. A bad `prefix` raises `ArgumentError` before any DDL.
- `new(opts)` takes exactly `repo`, `prefix`, `provider` (a module implementing `Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle.Provider`), `provider_account` (1..128 bytes), `deadline_ms` (the intent deadline after now, 1..86,400,000) and `clock`. It returns `{:ok, binding}` or `{:error, :invalid}`.
- `Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle.Provider`, the provider Need, has four callbacks, each called by this Piece at most once per call:
  - `charge(%{idempotency_key, payment_id, amount, recipient})` and `refund(%{idempotency_key, payment_id, refund_id, provider_ref, amount})` return `{:ok, %{provider_ref, state: :succeeded | :pending | :failed}}`, `{:declined, code}` (the provider refused it), `{:not_sent, reason}` (no byte reached the provider; an attestation of the implementation) or `{:unknown, :timeout | :transport}`;
  - `poll(%{idempotency_key, kind: :charge | :refund})` returns `{:ok, %{provider_ref, state}}`, `:no_effect` (the provider holds no operation with that key, final), `:pending` or `:unavailable`;
  - `verify_event(raw_body, headers)` returns `{:ok, event}` or `{:error, :invalid}`, with `event` `%{event_id, operation: {:charge, payment_id} | {:refund, payment_id, refund_id}, state: :succeeded | :pending | :failed, amount, provider_ref}`. It checks the provider's signature over the raw body before any parsing.

  `idempotency_key` is the intent's derived key. Codes, refs and event IDs are 1..256 bytes of valid UTF-8 without a control character.
- The effect-intent Adapters: a Binding fixes one operation, so the Piece has two, `Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle.ChargeAdapter` (operation `charge`) and `Elzoo.PaymentLifecycle.RefundAdapter` (operation `refund`), documented in the README with their Bindings. The logical reference of a charge is `<payment_id>/charge` and of a refund `<payment_id>/refund/<refund_id>`; the `operation_ref` of an observation is that logical reference. `arguments` are the canonical bytes of the tenant, the logical reference, the amount and the recipient. `send_effect/1` calls `charge` or `refund` with the request's key as `idempotency_key`, then writes the answer as the next observation row of its operation (its own transaction under the tenant scope, taking the payment row lock; a failure of that write changes the envelope only as the last sentence of this item says), and returns: for `{:ok, %{state: :succeeded | :failed}}` a completed envelope holding the canonical bytes of `%{provider_ref, state}`; for `{:ok, %{state: :pending}}` the declared tag `provider_pending`, `unknown / dispatched / unclassified`, so the intent stays dispatched and can be polled; for `{:declined, _}` `failed / dispatched / permanent`; for `{:not_sent, _}` an attested not-dispatched failure; for `{:unknown, _}` `timed_out / unknown` or `unknown / unknown`. `poll/1` calls `poll`, writes its answer as an observation the same way, and returns for `{:ok, %{state: :succeeded | :failed}}` a completed envelope, for `{:ok, %{state: :pending}}` and `:pending` the `pending` poll certificate, and for `:no_effect` and `:unavailable` the certificates of those names. When the observation write of a completed or declined answer fails, `send_effect/1` returns the declared tag `observation_unrecorded`, `unknown / dispatched / unclassified`, and `poll/1` the `pending` certificate, instead of the result above, so the intent stays dispatched, `fallback: :unknown` applies, and a later poll asks the provider again and records its answer; the Binding declares both tags.
- A **payment** is `%{payment_id, amount, recipient, approval_digest, state, provider_ref, refunded, revision}`: `payment_id` 1..100 bytes of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`; `amount` a positive `domain/money` amount; `recipient` 1..256 bytes; `state` `:created`, `:pending`, `:unknown`, `:succeeded` or `:failed`; `refunded` the sum of its refunds that are not `:failed`. A **refund** is `%{refund_id, amount, state, provider_ref}` with `refund_id` 1..64 bytes of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`, or the reserved `comp/charge` (the compensation's), and the same states without `:created`. No ID holds a `/` except that reserved one, so distinct operations have distinct logical references.
- `create(lc, tenant, %{payment_id, approved: %{amount, recipient}, approval_ref})` stores the payment `:created` with the approved amount and recipient and `approval_digest`, the SHA-256 of the canonical bytes of the approved amount's `Codec.encode/2` bytes, the recipient and `approval_ref` (1..256 bytes, the host's reference of the released approval). Idempotent by `payment_id`: equal bytes `{:ok, :duplicate}`, others `:conflict`.
- `charge(lc, tenant, %{payment_id, amount, recipient})` takes the action about to be sent. Its digest with the stored `approval_ref` must equal `approval_digest`, else `:approval_mismatch` (a changed amount, currency or recipient) with nothing written. From `:created` it sets `:pending` and returns `{:ok, admission}` (`%{key, identity, arguments, deadline}`); from `:pending` or `:unknown` it returns the same admission again, so the host recovers the same intent; from `:succeeded` or `:failed` it is `{:error, {:state, state}}`.
- `record(lc, tenant, %{operation, attempt, fallback})` applies the stored observation rows of an operation that are not yet applied, in `seq` order, whose attempt is at most `attempt`, the latest attempt number that the host read from the intent owner's `status/1`: `operation` `{:charge, payment_id}` or `{:refund, payment_id, refund_id}`; `fallback` `nil`, or `:unknown` when no row holds attempt `attempt` and the host read from `status/1` that the intent is dispatched, stranded or recovered read-only with no result (a crash after the send, or a failed observation write). Under the payment row lock each observation is a transition attempt below and is marked applied; when no row holds attempt `attempt` and `fallback` is `:unknown`, the operation takes the `{:unknown, _}` transition, whatever earlier attempts observed. It returns `{:ok, %{state, compensation}}`, with `compensation` as the compensation rule says; a repeat with nothing new writes nothing.
- `apply_event(lc, tenant, %{raw_body, headers})`: `raw_body` at most 65,536 bytes, `headers` 0..64 pairs. `verify_event` runs before any query; `{:error, :invalid}` (or a raise) is `:verification_failed` with nothing written. A verified event is stored once by `event_id` (a repeat with equal bytes changes nothing and returns `{:ok, %{duplicate: true, applied, state, compensation}}` with the stored `applied` and the operation's current state and compensation; other bytes are `:conflict`); under the payment row lock it applies the event's state to the operation as a transition, and returns `{:ok, %{applied, state, compensation}}`. An event for an unknown payment or refund, a state that is not a forward transition, or an amount or currency other than the operation's is stored with `applied: false` and changes nothing.
- `refund(lc, tenant, %{payment_id, refund_id, amount})`, `amount` a positive amount or `:remaining`; `comp/charge` is taken only with `:remaining` (another amount is `:invalid`), so no other refund can hold the compensation's ID. Under the payment row lock: the payment must be `:succeeded` (`{:state, s}` otherwise); a refund ID already stored with an equal request returns its admission again (`{:ok, admission}`), another request is `:conflict`. `held` is `refunded` (every refund not `:failed`, so a pending or unknown refund is held). `:remaining` is the payment's amount minus `held` at this call, fixed in the refund row, and `:nothing_to_refund` when it is zero; an explicit amount of another currency is `:currency`, and one with `held + amount` over the payment's amount is `:over_refund`. Otherwise the refund is stored `:pending` and its admission returned.
- `get(lc, tenant, %{payment_id})` returns `{:ok, %{payment, refunds, compensation}}`; `verify(lc)` checks the four tables with `Elzoo.TenantIsolation.verify/3`.

Errors are fixed atoms that never echo an ID, a recipient, a provider code or a body: `:invalid`, `:in_transaction`, `:isolation`, `:conflict`, `:unknown_payment`, `:approval_mismatch`, `{:state, state}`, `:currency`, `:over_refund`, `:nothing_to_refund`, `:verification_failed`, `:unavailable`.

## Rules

**Transitions** of a charge or a refund, from an observation (a send or poll answer), the fallback or a verified event; nothing else changes a state. A completed result below is an observation `{:ok, %{provider_ref, state}}`:
- `:created` or `:pending` or `:unknown` → `:succeeded` or `:failed` by a completed result or an event of that state, or by `{:declined, _}` (`:failed`);
- `:created` or `:unknown` → `:pending` by a completed result or an event with `state: :pending`;
- `:pending` → `:unknown` by `{:unknown, _}` (an observation or the fallback); `:unavailable` and a pending answer in `:pending` change nothing; `:no_effect` changes nothing for a charge, whose intent may then be dispatched again with the same key, and makes a refund `:failed`, which releases its hold (the provider holds no refund with that key, final; the host does not dispatch that refund's intent again, and another refund takes a new `refund_id`);
- `:succeeded` and `:failed` are final: a later result or event is recorded (an event) and changes nothing.

So a verified success event settles the payment row while its intent stays dispatched until the host polls it: only `poll/1` reports provider state to an intent. A dispatch after a recorded definite failure keeps the same intent, so the provider gets the same `idempotency_key`.

**Compensation (settled decision 1, Q10 A).** Whenever the charge is `:succeeded`, `record/3`, `apply_event/3` (also for a duplicate or an event with `applied: false`) and `get/3` return `compensation: %{kind: :refund, payment_id, refund_id: "comp/charge", amount: :remaining}`, not only the call whose transition made it succeed; while it is not, they return `compensation: nil`. So a caller that crashed after the transition, or retried after `:unavailable`, or whose step was settled first by an event, still gets it. The caller registers it with the step (in `assembly/ledger-checkout`, in the same transaction as the step's recorded result). Running it is `refund/3` with those values; running it twice is one refund, by its refund ID. Its amount is what is still refundable when it first runs, so after a partial refund it refunds the rest. A failed compensation is that refund's own recorded `:failed` or `:unknown` state.

Writes check shape and bounds (`:invalid`), then `repo.in_transaction?()` (`:in_transaction`) before any query, then run in `Elzoo.TenantIsolation.transact/3` with the READ COMMITTED check; every statement passes `log: false`; a database failure is `:unavailable`. `refund/3` takes the payment row with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` before it sums the refunds, so two concurrent refunds are serialized. Amounts are stored as their `Codec.encode/2` bytes and compared with `domain/money` only.

`piece.json` declares telemetry and control `not_applicable`, the config of the binding, and the dependencies `state/tenant-isolation`, `delivery/effect-intent` and `domain/money`; the README states the provider Need's Contract (the attestation of `{:not_sent, _}`, the key rule, signature verification before parsing) for the later provider Piece, and the minimum grants of the application role.

## Acceptance

Tests run on the worker's disposable PostgreSQL 16 with a `DBConnection.ConnectionPool` Repo in random schemas dropped afterwards, with the ready `delivery/effect-intent` owner over a volatile Store acknowledged for the tests, and a scripted provider that records every call with its `idempotency_key`.

1. RC-24, timeout after charge: the scripted charge times out, `record/3` makes the payment `:unknown`, `poll/1` reports `succeeded`, and `record/3` makes it `:succeeded` with the compensation; the provider saw one charge. The send answer and the poll answer of attempt 1 are two rows, each applied once. A repeated `record/3` and `get/3` return the same compensation. A charge answered `pending` keeps the intent dispatched; a later poll `succeeded` settles it. A crash after the send with no observation stored: `record/3` with `fallback: :unknown` makes it `:unknown`. A succeeded charge whose observation write fails (the Repo refuses that one transaction): the intent stays dispatched with `observation_unrecorded`, `record/3` with `fallback: :unknown` makes the payment `:unknown`, and a later poll `succeeded` settles it with the compensation; the provider saw one charge.
2. A verified success event before any poll settles the row `:succeeded` with the compensation, while the intent's `status/1` still shows it dispatched; a later `record/3` of the checkout's step, with nothing new to apply, also returns the compensation; a later poll result is recorded and changes nothing.
3. A dispatch after a definite failure (a `{:not_sent, _}` send, then `:rearm` and `:dispatch`; and a poll `:no_effect`, then a new dispatch) carries the same `idempotency_key`, equal to the key of the first dispatch.
4. Two concurrent refunds behind a barrier (another connection holds the payment row lock until both are seen waiting in `pg_locks`) that together pass the amount: one is stored, the other is `:over_refund`. A pending and an unknown refund are held.
5. A duplicate event (`duplicate: true`, no change, and the compensation when the charge is succeeded), a not-sent first attempt followed by a crash in the second send (`record/3` with `attempt` 2 and `fallback: :unknown` makes it `:unknown`), an out-of-order event (`failed` after `succeeded`, recorded with `applied: false`), an event of another amount (recorded, no change) and an event for an unknown payment.
6. An event that fails verification (`verify_event` refuses a changed body) changes nothing and stores nothing.
7. A changed approved amount, currency or recipient at `charge/3` is `:approval_mismatch` with nothing written and no intent admitted.
8. The compensation run twice is one refund; after a partial refund of 30 of 100, the compensation refunds 70; after a full refund it is `:nothing_to_refund`; a compensation whose refund fails leaves that refund `:failed`, with the payment `:succeeded`; a declined refund whose observation write fails, then a poll `:no_effect`, makes that refund `:failed` and releases its hold, so a later refund of the whole remaining amount is stored.
9. `create/3` and `refund/3` idempotency and conflict; `refund/3` of a payment that is not `:succeeded`; an explicit refund in another currency; a `payment_id` or a `refund_id` holding `/`, and `comp/charge` with an explicit amount, are `:invalid`.
10. Row security as `elzoo_app` (ELZ-285): `verify/1` passes; under T1 a T2 payment is `:unknown_payment` for `charge/3`, `record/3`, `refund/3` and `get/3`, `create/3` with a T2 `payment_id` creates a T1 payment and leaves the T2 row unchanged, and a verified event naming a T2 payment is stored for T1 with `applied: false`; a direct insert of a T2 row into each table fails with SQLSTATE 42501; every function succeeds with only the README grants. ## Limits to state

No provider is named or exercised: the evidence is a scripted provider, so no claim about a provider's settlement, idempotency or event semantics; the ELZ-025 key rule must be checked against the named provider's documented idempotency in `integration/payments`. The approved terms, the approval reference, the provider Need's attestations and signature check, and the host's `fallback` reading of an intent with no observation are host Needs. After `:no_effect` with the intent's deadline passed, or a stranded intent, the payment stays `:pending` or `:unknown`: no new intent is made by this Piece, and an operator resolves it outside it. A balanced ledger does not prove settlement. The `state/tenant-isolation` limits apply; READ COMMITTED is a consumer Need. No authorize/capture, split settlement, disputes or multi-currency settlement.

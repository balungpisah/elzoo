# Wave 9 — More tools, doors, models and money

## Status and scope

This document is planning input for Wave 9 (`More tools, doors, models and money`) of the P13 track in [bot-platform.md](bot-platform.md). It promotes nothing to ready and publishes no Piece task. [release-plan.json](release-plan.json) remains the one canonical planning source; handles are provisional until publication, and the coordinator assigns the ELZ IDs then. The levels below are published as tasks after the owner answers the questions at the end ("Provider prerequisites and open questions"); each published task is then the Contract of its Piece, and where it differs from a sketch below, the task wins.

Wave 8 is accepted: its root consumer acceptance [ELZ-349](../../tasks/ELZ-349-wave8-bots-talk-acceptance.md) is merged on `main` (`2a02e1e5`), and its closure is recorded in [Wave 8 closure](bot-platform-wave8.md#wave-8-closure-and-open-handoff) by the task of this plan ([ELZ-355](../../tasks/ELZ-355-wave9-plan.md)). The owner gave a standing go to plan and build the remaining waves. Wave 9 is the last row of the wave table of [bot-platform.md](bot-platform.md); "What remains after Wave 9" at the end lists what is left for Elzoo to be complete, without planning it. This plan treats the Wave 9 rows of [bot-platform.md](bot-platform.md) ("Waves 4 to 9" and "Subsequent waves (Waves 6–9)") as a proposal, checks each handle against the ready manifests, and keeps a new Piece only for missing policy. Where a ready Piece lacks something that clearly belongs to it, the plan extends it in place under its ID (rule of 2026-11-14; `AGENTS.md` section 4).

Wave 9 means four things. **Tools:** a bot gets OAuth credentials for the tools it calls, and a model may write a short program that calls tools (code mode). **Doors:** other systems reach Elzoo's bots over MCP, and Elzoo sends signed events out to them. **Models:** more model providers, images as model input, and a routing rule that picks an allowed provider and moves to another one after a refusal, each move a separate, counted Attempt. **Money:** a subscription renews per period, a period becomes a numbered invoice, a payment goes through a provider lifecycle with unknown outcomes reconciled, and a checkout that pays an invoice or tops up a wallet undoes its completed steps in reverse order when a later step fails.

The plan proposes **10 new Pieces (11 with `integration/payments` under Q9 A, B or C) and 6 in-place extensions of ready Pieces across 2 dependency levels, plus a mandatory root consumer acceptance at Level 3**, from 24 candidate handles, with one conditional provider prerequisite (the payment provider of Q9). The four parts are independent until Level 3, so the coordinator can publish Level 1 in batches within the worker limits of `AGENTS.md` section 3. Departures from the pruned baseline, each argued in "All candidate handles":

1. `workflow/code-mode` is a composition, not a Piece: the ready `workflow/lua-tool` already takes its Lua source from the host's nested binding, so a model-written program is a tool call whose binding builds that spec. Q1 decides the language.
2. `integration/ai-multimodal` is absorbed into `integration/ai-text` in place (image parts by artifact reference) and into each provider that declares image input.
3. `integration/ai-model-catalogue` is absorbed: the locked `llm_db` is the catalogue, and `AGENTS.md` section 4 forbids a wrapper around an ordinary upstream API.
4. `delivery/model-routing` (RC-25) is a pure selection and after-failure rule; the new Attempt after a refusal is asked by `workflow/agent-loop`, extended in place.
5. `domain/plan` and `domain/invoice` move from "deferred" in the pruned row into Level 1, by the owner's 2026-11-21 plan-types decision ("Subscriptions wait for Wave 9").
6. The saga of the settled decision lives in `assembly/ledger-checkout`, and `domain/payment-lifecycle` declares the compensation of each of its steps; no separate saga Piece (Q10).

### Settled owner decisions

These decisions of [bot-platform.md](bot-platform.md#owner-decisions-that-are-still-open) are inputs of this plan, not questions. Every sketch below keeps them.

1. **Wave 9 saga rules (owner, 2026-11-21).** "`assembly/ledger-checkout` and `domain/payment-lifecycle` register each compensation with the step that succeeded. Compensations run in reverse order by default and in parallel only where declared. Each compensation is idempotent with its own key, and a failed compensation is its own recorded outcome."
2. **Wave 9 retry delay from the failure (owner, 2026-11-21).** "`delivery/retry-timeout` gains, in place, a retry delay taken from the failure, such as a Retry-After value or a rate-limit wait, plus optional jitter, for results that were not dispatched only; a possibly dispatched result keeps today's rule. First caller: Wave 9 `delivery/model-routing`. A published task changes the ready Contract."
3. **Plan types and subscriptions (owner, 2026-11-21).** "Pay-as-you-go, quota-capped and prepaid are compositions of Pieces, not Pieces or Assemblies … Subscriptions wait for Wave 9 (`domain/plan`, `domain/invoice`)." They compose the Wave 6 `domain/metering`, `domain/pricing`, `domain/quota`, `domain/entitlement`, `domain/journal` and `domain/money`. The billing model is both postpaid and prepaid (Wave 6 Q5 C), each rate card has a currency and a required `rounding` (Wave 6 Q3), and there is no platform currency.
4. **RC-59 (owner, 2026-11-12).** "Tenant-added servers need a new one-server runtime-endpoint Piece under integration/egress-policy in wave 5, bearer first; OAuth with integration/oauth-client waits for wave 9. Lists belong to server and credential … future per-person credentials require separate lists." Wave 5 delivered the bearer form as `integration/mcp-runtime` (ELZ-283).
5. **No corner (owner, 2026-11-21, standing).** Every Wave 9 Contract leaves each deferred option addable in place before the first release without breaking existing callers: closed sets that can gain members, records and results that can gain keys, no single currency, provider or plan shape. Each sketch lists what it keeps open.

**A reading of decision 2, recorded for the owner to correct.** Under `integration/ai-text`'s Contract amendment 1, a provider's `throttled` (429) and `overloaded` (529 or 503) are "failed / dispatched / retryable" and "not redispatched by default" ([ai-text README](../../pieces/integration/ai-text/README.md), lines 61 and 66–83): the request reached transport. They carry the provider's `retry_after`, but they are possibly dispatched, so by decision 2 the new delay does not apply to them and they keep today's rule (`:unresolved`, never sent again). The not-dispatched results that carry a delay are local: `delivery/rate-limit`'s `:tenant_rate` and `:principal_rate` refusals with `retry_after_ms`, and `credentials_unavailable` (no delay, so the jittered backoff). The coordinator therefore reads the provider's `retry_after` as an input of `delivery/model-routing`: a cool-down of that candidate before any **new** Attempt on it (a new logical request, as `assembly/knowledge-ingest` already does for a throttled embedding call), never a redispatch of the first request. A cool-down is not a retry, so it does not contradict decision 2.

Its Cure `classify` reads a DeliveryResult in this order: `completed`, `error_class` `permanent`, `dispatch` `not_dispatched` (retryable), else dispatched ([retry_policy.cure](../../pieces/delivery/retry-timeout/cure/elzoo/retry_policy.cure), lines 19–24). ### Level 1 — independent Pieces and in-place extensions (disjoint, parallelizable)

**`delivery/retry-timeout`, in place (settled decision 2).**

4. **Money:** a subscription with a trial renews at its period end (a 31 January anchor gives a 28 February end); its fee line and a priced usage charge make one invoice, finalized with the next number, its receivable posted once; a checkout pays it: the charge is pending, a duplicate and an out-of-order provider event change nothing, the verified success settles the receivable and marks the invoice paid; a checkout whose invoice step fails compensates in reverse order (journal reversal, then refund), each once; a failed compensation stops `needs_operator`; two refunds that together pass the amount are refused one; a charge that times out is unknown until `poll`; a changed approved amount is refused; a prepaid top-up posts to a no-negative wallet.
5. **Isolation:** as `elzoo_app`, owner-only DDL on each new Piece table is refused; a read without a tenant predicate finds each tenant's rows only in its own scope; a write of another tenant's row is refused with SQLSTATE 42501.
6. **Negative controls**, one at a time, each restored before the final passing run: connect without `SET ROLE` (with the guard off for that run only, group 5's owner-only refusals must fail); register the webhook endpoint without the egress check in the harness (group 2's private-address case must fail); set `on_unknown` to fall back in the harness route (group 3's unknown case must fail); register the checkout compensations in forward order in the harness declaration (group 4's order case must fail); build the code-mode spec with every registry tool instead of the declared functions (group 1's undeclared-function case must fail).

Limits to state: scripted models and provider, so no claim about answer quality, provider behaviour or settlement; local test servers, not a live OAuth provider, MCP host, webhook receiver or payment provider; pinned test identities; the Lua sandbox is not OS isolation (Q1); a retried MCP call starts a second Run; no tax or discount; no channel adapter; the RunEngine Store tables, the recipe-store stream and the artifact records are not under row security; the harness code named above.

## Provider prerequisites and open questions

**Provider prerequisites.** Under the recommended answers, only Q9 can add one: if the owner names Stripe, adopting `stripity_stripe` (3.3.3 in the dispositions) is its own provider task with the full check, merged before `integration/payments`; for Xendit or Midtrans the provider task first checks Hex for a maintained client and otherwise records that the Piece uses Req directly. No PostgreSQL extension or CI service is added. Under Q1 B a JavaScript sandbox is a native provider task; under Q3 C an OAuth library is one. The test servers use the locked `plug_cowboy`.

**Owner questions.** Each has options and a recommendation.

1. **Q1 — The language of model-written code** (code mode; the open decision "not registered (wave 9)" of bot-platform.md).
   - A: Lua in the ready `runtime/lua-sandbox`, as a composition over `workflow/lua-tool`; the sandbox is a separate process with no `os`, `io` or `load` and hard bounds, but not OS isolation, which is stated as a limit.
   - B: JavaScript in a new sandbox (for example QuickJS) as a new runtime Piece with its own native provider task.
   - C: no code mode in Wave 9.

   *Recommendation: A.* It needs no new Piece or dependency, and every effect of the program is still an ordinary tool call under the Run's authority. Models write JavaScript or Python more fluently, so B stays addable as a second runtime; OS confinement of the sandbox stays open either way.
2. **Q2 — Whose OAuth tokens** (`integration/oauth-client`, `integration/mcp-runtime`).
   - A: tenant connections: client credentials, and an authorization code with PKCE that a tenant admin consents once; tool lists stay keyed by tenant and server.
   - B: A plus per-person connections, with mcp-runtime keying lists by tenant, server and person (a new list Contract under RC-59).

   *Recommendation: A.* It serves a tenant's tool servers without changing RC-59's lists; per-person tokens are the first need of `integration/connector-drive`, which is deferred, and a `subject` key stays addable.
3. **Q3 — How the OAuth exchange is made** (`integration/oauth-client`).
   - A: through the locked Req, Jason and OTP `:crypto`; the Piece writes only the token lifecycle (when to refresh, single flight, sealing, revocation), and no reader is written by hand.
   - B: through `ex_mcp`'s `ExMCP.Authorization` modules (pinned 1.8.1), which bring their own HTTP and token-store modules; their retry and storage behaviour would be checked first.
   - C: a new library (for example `oauth2`), its own provider task.

   *Recommendation: A.* The wire formats are parsed by libraries that Elzoo already adopts; the lifecycle policy is what no library gives under the tenant floor and the no-hidden-retry rule of `delivery/effect-intent`.
4. **Q4 — What Elzoo's MCP server exposes** (`integration/mcp-server`).
   - A: each bot that the verified caller may run, as one tool; a call is one Run with a bounded wait.
   - B: the tenant's own tools directly, as a proxy outside a Run.
   - C: no MCP server in Wave 9.

   *Recommendation: A.* A call then gets a Run's budget, authority, approvals and record; B would run tools outside the engine's checks.
5. **Q5 — How events go out** (`integration/webhook-out`).
   - A: the Standard Webhooks headers and HMAC-SHA-256; after a possibly dispatched failure, a new counted try with the same `webhook-id` only for an endpoint registered as deduplicating, else a dead-letter.
   - B: mirror `integration/webhook-in`'s `x-elzoo-signature` scheme, with the same redelivery rule.

   *Recommendation: A.* Receivers have libraries for that format; the redelivery rule keeps the no-blind-resend rule of `delivery/effect-intent`.
6. **Q6 — Which model providers are added** (`integration/ai-text-google`, `integration/ai-text-compatible`).
   - A: both.
   - B: Google only.
   - C: OpenAI-compatible only.
   - D: neither; routing works over the two ready providers.

   *Recommendation: A.* A third vendor gives routing a real choice, and a compatible endpoint lets a tenant use a self-hosted or regional model, which serves data-residency rules; both run over the locked `req_llm`.
7. **Q7 — Which media go into a model** (`integration/ai-text` and the providers).
   - A: images only (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF), by artifact reference.
   - B: images and PDF documents.
   - C: none in Wave 9.

   *Recommendation: A.* Images are the common bot input and every chosen provider takes them; documents differ more between providers and stay addable as a part kind.
8. **Q8 — Fallback after an unknown model outcome** (`delivery/model-routing`, `workflow/agent-loop`).
   - A: no fallback after `unknown`: a new Attempt follows only a refusal, and the first Attempt's possible cost is recorded.
   - B: fallback after `unknown` too, as a declared route option, accepting that two answers and two costs may result.

   *Recommendation: A.* An unknown first Attempt may already have produced output and cost; B stays addable as `on_unknown: :fallback`.
9. **Q9 — The payment provider** (`integration/payments`, `domain/payment-lifecycle`; the pruned row prefers no-custody or sub-account split settlement).
   - A: Stripe (Connect for split settlement).
   - B: Xendit (sub-accounts).
   - C: Midtrans.
   - D: none named yet: the provider-neutral lifecycle and the checkout are built, the root acceptance uses a scripted provider binding, and `integration/payments` waits.

   *Recommendation: the provider of the first paying consumer, with a test account; if none is named, D.* RC-24 says "Choose provider before binding semantics", and the ELZ-025 key rule must be checked against that provider's documented idempotency.
10. **Q10 — Where the saga runs** (`assembly/ledger-checkout`, `domain/payment-lifecycle`).
    - A: `assembly/ledger-checkout` keeps the durable saga log; `domain/payment-lifecycle` returns the compensation of each of its succeeded steps, which the caller registers.
    - B: a new `workflow/saga` Piece used by both.

    *Recommendation: A.* There is one saga caller in Wave 9, and the settled rules hold either way; B stays addable when a second caller appears.
11. **Q11 — Subscriptions and invoices** (`domain/plan`, `domain/invoice`).
    - A: monthly and yearly UTC periods from an anchor, trial, change at the next period with no proration, cancel at period end or now with no refund; invoices of one currency with fee, usage and adjustment lines, gapless numbers per series, credit notes, an incomplete charge refused, no tax or discount.
    - B: A plus tax and discount lines.
    - C: A with proration of a plan change.

    *Recommendation: A.* It covers a recurring plan end to end with the fewest rules; tax depends on the consumer's jurisdiction and stays addable as a line kind, and proration as a change option.
12. **Q12 — Channel adapters** (`integration/channel-*`).
    - A: none in Wave 9; each comes when a consumer names it.
    - B: Telegram first (a secret-token header and a bot API over HTTPS).
    - C: Slack first.
    - D: WhatsApp first (Meta app review and message templates).

    *Recommendation: A.* No channel is named active in the sources, and the first-party web channel and MCP are Wave 9's doors; B is the cheapest if the owner wants one now.

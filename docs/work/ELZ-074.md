# ELZ-074 — http-job: the shared wait helper is wall-clock bounded

Task copy: `tasks/ELZ-074-http-job-wait-wall-clock.md` (issue #137). Test-only fix-forward on a merged test helper. The owner delegated the choice of repair to the main coordinator on 2026-11-09 at 13:20 WIB ("Pick which one u think is best"); the main coordinator chose this option (strict assertions kept, wall-clock bounded wait) over the investigator's weaker assertion.

## Mechanism

Investigation report (independent investigator, Codex, read-only toward the repository; original held by the pieces coordinator; SHA-256 `8db3c20e3274e5bb84d38027f4d4bcd6fa917b3e282fc47a345ad1f082cdef20`): in the hosted failure of `live Oban rescue and concurrent wakeups still reserve one physical send` (check-hosted run 42556924695, aggregate copied consumer), the owner had made exactly one durable reservation (`{:Dispatched, :Fresh, 1}`, attempts 1) and no `PUT` had started when the final wait expired. The asynchronous send task, started with `Task.Supervisor.async_nolink` in the one-child `sends` supervisor, was starved of scheduler time under CPU load. The helper `wait/2` was 300 iterations of a 10 ms sleep: about 3 s nominal, but not a wall-clock bound (22.8 s elapsed in the instrumented failure). Not a slow `PUT`, not pool pressure, not a failed rescue; neither the Adapter nor the owner misbehaved. Alternatives tried by the investigator and rejected: a longer iteration count (600 fails under stress; 3000 fails after 70 s), a deterministic clock (the injected clock drives policy time only), a larger test-only Adapter budget (the starvation happens before any call).

## Change

`pieces/assembly/http-job/test/elzoo/http_job_test.exs`: `wait/2` becomes a monotonic wall-clock deadline (default `@wait_ms 60_000`, 10 ms sleep between polls); on the deadline the final poll is asserted with the message `Expected truthy, got false after <elapsed> ms (wall clock)`. The two callers that passed `600` iterations (twice the old default) now pass `120_000` ms (twice the new default); every other caller keeps the default. No assertion changes: the concurrent-wakeups test still requires `{:Confirmed, 1}` and exactly one `PUT`, so a stalled send task still fails the test, and a slow but progressing runner passes. The helper moved the later lines down by eleven; the two assertions formerly at 582–583 are unchanged in content.

## Why the strict assertions stay

The property the test names is one physical send under two live wakes, confirmed. Loosening it to "at most one PUT and one attempt" would stop proving that the reserved attempt is carried out. The wall-clock bound removes the dependence on scheduler fairness of a finite poll loop without weakening the property.

## Evidence

Recorded in the PR body: the Piece suite at the PR head; 20 runs of the tests at lines 472 and 555 at baseline and 20 under a moderate load (`ERL_FLAGS="+S 4:4"` with four CPU busy loops); one run under eight busy loops recorded as information.

## Limits

The bound does not make a starved BEAM deliver promptly; a runner that cannot schedule the send task within 60 s still fails this test, which is the intended signal. Adapter budgets, the owner and Oban are unchanged.

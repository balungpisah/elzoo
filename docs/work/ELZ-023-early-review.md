## 3. Prioritised findings

### F1. HIGH (owner decision): replay-safe ground narrowed relative to the live ground 2
Grounding: task Admission 2 (ground 2 and "a bare `failed/dispatched` result satisfies neither"). The live text is internally ambiguous: if ground 2 is "an idempotency contract", a bare `failed/dispatched` plus the contract would satisfy it, which contradicts "satisfies neither". The proposal resolves the ambiguity toward the narrow reading without saying so. Required: root chooses (see R-B). Do not decide inside implementation.

### F2. HIGH (evidence gate): the probe does not establish, and cannot express, the headline invariant
- The probe state `Intended` carries no history, so "the first dispatch is not a re-arm" and "every re-arm passes through `failed_definite`" cannot be stated (my probe P4: the decision for the first `Intended+Arm` and for `Intended+Arm` after `NoSend → Definite → Rearm` is identical).
- The four checked laws cover only: no dispatch from `Dispatched`; the ground of the state after `Dispatched`; `Stranded` is terminal; one unknown-without-replay row. The central safety statement ("dispatch only from `Intended`+`Arm`", "`Intended` after a possible send implies a recorded ground") is only a finite assertion over a table that does not assert the next phase for most events.
- Empirical result (my P3, same Cure pin, exit 0): three policy mutants in which `Dispatched` goes back to `Intended` on `Pending`, on `Expired`, or on `Resume` **pass all four laws, pass the probe's finite table, and pass its positive assertions**. They are exactly the forbidden silent re-arm. Only a mutant that changes `Lost` was caught, by one positive assertion by luck. The probe's three proof-rejecting mutations are real but do not cover these.
- Feasible fix (my scratch probe, exit 0, clean audit: no axioms, holes, opaque, unresolved, unaudited, non-total; absurd 0): carry provenance in the state, `Intended(Fresh | After(ground))`, and prove three one-step label laws against separately written truth tables: `dispatch_only_after_arm`, `fresh_only_from_empty`, `after_only_from_definite`. No trace induction is needed; the trace statement follows because the label is part of the state. Six mutants (`Pending`/`Expired` to `Intended(Fresh)`, `Pending` to `Intended(After(wrong))`, `Admit` dispatches, `Rearm` with a wrong ground, `Rearm` to `Fresh`) are all rejected by the laws (`conversion_failure` at the named law). Files: `/tmp/elz023-review/gen.py`, `label.cure`, `label.exs`, `labelmut.exs`.
- Gate for implementation: the production state must carry the rearm ground and attempt counter inside the phase value the law quantifies over; the production negative controls must include the silent-return mutants (`Pending`, `Lost`, `Resume`, `Expired`, timeout, `failed/dispatched` to `intended`) and an always-stop mutant that fails a mandatory positive scenario. The reduced probe's `Rearm` is unconditional and ignores deadline, disarm and capability validity; those must be in the production policy, not only in Elixir.

### F3. HIGH: results for an old attempt and contradictory evidence are unspecified
Grounding: task Scope ("accept a late result at most once and only while `dispatched`; … a refused late result is retained as evidence"); proposal table last row ("`confirmed` or `stranded` … unchanged") and D5 scenario "delayed old-attempt result after rearm". The proposal lists the scenario but not the outcome. Cases that decide safety:
1. A correlated `completed` for attempt k arrives after a no-send or no-effect ground for attempt k was recorded, or after the intent re-armed. The real effect exists; the ground's premise was false. Treating it as "stale evidence, state unchanged" leaves the intent re-armable and can cause a second effect.
2. Under `provider_replay_safe`, a completed old attempt with the same key is the same operation and should settle.
Required rule (owner decision, R-C): a validated `completed` for any attempt of this intent after a `failed_definite` ground, or while a later attempt is `dispatched`, is either (a) settlement of the same operation (replay-safe ground only) or (b) a recorded contradiction that disarms rearm until operator reconciliation. And: if that evidence cannot be retained (capacity or Store failure), rearm must stay blocked.

### F4. HIGH (owner decision R-D): evidence retention and bounds, details missing
- The promise is weakened (see D6). If admitted, specify: (i) which evidence is guaranteed retained (decisive settlement, any contradiction, the first refused result per attempt) and which is only counted (routine `pending`/unavailable poll observations are host-driven and can exhaust 1024 records); (ii) a per-evidence-record byte cap (without it, 1024 records do not fit a 1 MiB model next to a 64 KiB argument and a 40-byte-per-sequence seen index); (iii) the numeric size of the "reserved settlement space" and when it is checked; (iv) whether 1 MiB is per snapshot or cumulative: checkpoints are immutable rows per sequence, so cumulative storage is O(transitions × snapshot size) unless retention maintenance prunes them (`integration/sql` has bounded retention; the proposal says "no automatic pruning"); (v) a start-time check that the supplied Store limits satisfy the Piece's needs (`max_record_bytes` at least 64 KiB plus framing, `max_checkpoint_bytes` at least 1 MiB, `max_batch_size`); the accepted Store defaults are consumer-chosen (the probe uses 4096 and 65,536).
- A refused (capacity) append of a refusal record must itself be host-visible; the proposal says so, keep it.

### F5. MEDIUM: "recovery deadline" is a new, undefined concept
Task Admission 2 has one input, "the deadline", and says only "a crash or deadline by itself never re-arms". The proposal adds `dispatched → stranded` at a "recovery deadline". Define who supplies it (trusted binding configuration), its relation to the Envelope `deadline_unix_ms` (the Envelope says expiry decisions are ELZ-006's and no clock-skew guarantee), and a default of "none"; otherwise an intent dispatched shortly before the admission deadline becomes `stranded` at once. Also state the interaction with "accept a late result only while `dispatched`": after `stranded` a late genuine `completed` is refused (evidence retained), which is acceptable only if the operator path (F6) can record it.

### F6. MEDIUM: the `stranded` operator path is not specified
Acceptance bullet 3 requires "a documented operator path". The proposal exposes a reconciliation ticket but no way to record an outcome. Minimum text: `stranded` stays terminal for dispatch; the operator verifies at the provider and the host records the business outcome itself; if the effect did not happen the host starts a **new** logical request identity (new key), never a rearm of the same key; an optional bounded operator-evidence record may be appended without changing the phase. README must say that creating a new request after an unverified `stranded` risks a double effect.

### F7. MEDIUM: ownership, fencing and the pause-after-reservation case
The proposal says the host must fence one live owner per key. Reading the design, the real send gate is the CAS: every send requires a committed `dispatched` record at an expected revision, so two owners cannot both authorise a send from the same record, and a recovered record can only poll. What fencing still protects is the scenario of owner A that committed `dispatched` and then paused before the send, while owner B recovers, polls, and (under replay-safe or a fenced no-effect certificate) re-arms and sends; A then sends late. That must be a mandatory scenario, with the expected result by ground: replay-safe is harmless by the provider contract; without a capability B stranded, A's late completion is refused and retained. Reword the Need so it does not claim that host fencing is the dispatch safety mechanism; the certificate must fence the old attempt, as D3 says.

### F8. MEDIUM: correlation inputs and reuse of the Runner's classes
- `Elzoo.Envelope.validate_result/1` accepts a result with no `attempt_id` (my P1: accepted). The Envelope README says attempt fields are optional and "late-result matching is ELZ-006/007 policy". The owner must refuse an observation without `attempt_id` equal to a recorded attempt and a matching message/correlation id.
- `Elzoo.RetryTimeout.classify/1` returns `:permanent` for `failed/dispatched/permanent` and `timed_out/dispatched/permanent` (my P2). The intent policy must poll those (the proposal says so); it must not reuse `classify` for dispatched rows or for disarm. Adapters that wrap the ready Runner must be in `redispatch_forbidden` mode with a single attempt, otherwise the Runner's retry loop is the transport re-send the task lists as a dangerous path. Add to the conformance Need.

### F9. MEDIUM: provider key lifetime and revision
- Anchor the replay validity horizon at the earliest possibly-sent attempt's `dispatched` record; require horizon ≥ the Adapter's maximum in-flight lifetime plus clock-skew allowance, so a delayed original cannot arrive after the key expired; name the clock.
- After an argument revision the same provider key is reused with different arguments. Providers either reject or return the cached result. The binding must declare which, and a cached-result provider must make revision refusal mandatory. The revision is legal only after `adapter_no_send` for all earlier attempts, which is again Adapter-attested.
- Retention Need: records of confirmed/stranded/dispatched intents must be kept at least as long as the provider's idempotency horizon and the business duplicate-request horizon; pruning a terminal stream makes a repeated admission a new intent and repeats the effect. State it in the README and the Contract Needs.

### F10. MEDIUM (root confirmation): two scope readings
1. Task Scope says "define the store behaviour and a test double; bind to the ELZ-009 provider". The proposal reuses the accepted `Elzoo.DurableLog.Store` behaviour and `Memory`, and adds only an intent-specific binding, fold and conformance fixtures. I think this is the right engineering choice (a parallel behaviour would duplicate CAS and uncertainty rules), but it does not literally "define the store behaviour". Root should confirm the reading and record it in the amendment comment.
2. "Checkpoint-before-send" is read as append **and** checkpoint acknowledged. The append alone already gives the safety property (a recovered `dispatched` can only poll). The stricter reading matches the title and is acceptable, but it creates a case with committed `dispatched` and a rejected checkpoint (for example `checkpoint_too_large`): the intent is never sent and can only end `stranded` unless replay-safe. Reserve checkpoint capacity before the append (ties to F4).

### F11. LOW (record in the plan)
- Fresh-nonce fence refinement (optional): ELZ-009 allows a competing fresh-nonce CAS at the original expected revision. For an indeterminate **dispatch reservation** this can prove the reservation never committed (the competing record supersedes it) and hence the owner never sent; this could be a fourth, safe route back to `intended` instead of waiting for poll. Not needed for safety; stranding is safe.
- One GenServer per key does not scale to many refunds; CAS already serialises state. Keep the owner process ephemeral and do not promise a registry.
- Passing the volatile `Memory` double as the production Store satisfies "a Store was supplied". Consider requiring an explicit acknowledgement for a non-durable Store.
- Telemetry/Config/Control: the proposal defers them; at ready time they must be declared per `docs/operational-contracts.md` (config fixed at owner start is correct; telemetry must not carry payment references).
- Sensitive data: arguments (up to 64 KiB, may contain payment references) are persisted in the event log in clear; storage access control and classification are consumer Needs.
- Dependencies: not depending on `delivery/retry-timeout` is reasonable since its classes are not reused (F8); the PR should say why it differs from "Depends on ELZ-006 failure classes and deadlines".

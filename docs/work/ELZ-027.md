## Contract reading

The slice is the pure decision between an already computed due slot and one
admissible occurrence. Due-instant computation, timezone mapping, the clock,
transport, job execution and persistence stay outside the piece.

**Inputs per admission call.** The retained state, a list of due slots (UTC
Unix milliseconds) and an arrival record `{now_unix_ms, available_since_unix_ms}`.
`available_since_unix_ms` is the availability boundary of A1/A1-rev2: the
caller establishes that the scheduler was continuously available from it
through `now_unix_ms` and that every unrecorded due slot before it passed
while offline. Validation (Elixir boundary, before any transition): slots are
integers in non-decreasing order; every slot is `<= now_unix_ms` (a later
slot refuses the whole batch as `not_due`); `available_since_unix_ms` is an
integer `<= now_unix_ms`; a missing or malformed boundary refuses the call.
Nothing is classified when the call is refused. The piece cannot verify the
availability predicate; the README states it as the caller's obligation.

**Decision per slot, in order.**

1. `slot <= last_recorded_unix_ms`: `already_recorded{slot}`; state
   unchanged. Under the complete-sequence assumption the slot was decided
   before; the piece does not know whether it was admitted, skipped or decided
   under an older generation and asserts no occurrence. This is the second
   activation of one slot.
2. `slot < available_since_unix_ms`: offline catch-up slot. Under `skip` and
   `replace`: `skipped{slot, offline_catch_up}`, watermark advances to the
   slot, unsettled set unchanged. Under `queue(n)`: admitted as
   `occurrence{id, offline_catch_up}` while fewer than `n` are unsettled,
   otherwise `skipped{slot, queue_full}`; watermark advances either way.
3. Otherwise the slot is live and the overlap policy decides: `skip` with an
   unsettled occurrence gives `skipped{slot, overlap}`; `replace` with an
   unsettled occurrence gives `replaced{old id, new id}` and the new one is
   the only unsettled occurrence; `queue(n)` as in step 2 with origin `live`;
   an empty unsettled set gives `occurrence{id, live}`. The watermark
   advances to the slot in every case.

**Identity.** The identity is the record `OccurrenceId{schedule_id,
generation, scheduled_unix_ms}` produced by the production function
`identify(sid, gen, slot)`. Arrival instant, boundary, node, process and
delivery id are not inputs of that function. `encode_id/1` is a derived Elixir
text key `"<generation>:<instant>:<schedule id>"` for consumers that need text;
`decode_id/1` inverts it; the round trip is tested, not proved.

**Watermark.** `last_recorded_unix_ms` is supplied at construction (the caller
decides and records it, for example the definition instant) and advances to
every decided slot. It is per schedule, not per generation: a redefinition
keeps it, so a slot decided under an older generation is not re-run under the
new one. The caller presents the complete ordered sequence (assumption).

**Settlement.** `{acknowledged | released, id}` removes a matching unsettled
occurrence and records the kind. An id of an older generation is
`rejected{stale_generation}`; an id that is not unsettled is
`rejected{unknown_occurrence}`. Both change nothing. Neither cancels work
already dispatched.

**Delivery admission.** `{id, payload}` is a query: `admitted` only when the
id is unsettled and the payload equals the occurrence payload; otherwise
`rejected{stale_generation | unknown_occurrence | payload_mismatch}`. State
never changes, so a retry presents the same id and keeps its identity.

**Redefinition.** Same schedule id and a strictly greater generation replace
the schedule, empty the unsettled set and record every displaced id as
`redefined{old generation, [ids]}`. Equal or smaller generation
`rejected{stale_generation}`, different id `rejected{schedule_mismatch}`,
invalid definition `rejected{invalid_schedule}`; all three change nothing. The
generation is the expected-revision check of a configuration write; the piece
compares, the owner serializes and commits (assumption).

**Serialized state ownership.** Deterministic ids do not serialize
activations. Transitions must be applied by one owner at a time (a process, or
a store with compare-and-set such as ELZ-009 when composed). The test
exercises a serialized test owner and sequential replay only; no concurrent or
exactly-once claim.

**Composition (as written in the issue).** Settlement from a consumer arrives as a
`core/envelope` DeliveryResult whose `correlation_id` is `encode_id(id)`:
`settlement_from_result/1` validates it with `Elzoo.Envelope.validate_result/1`
and maps `completed` to acknowledged, `rejected` and `failed` to released, and
`timed_out` and `unknown` to no settlement (the occurrence stays unsettled; an
unknown external outcome is never treated as no effect). Repeated admitted
deliveries of one occurrence are suppressed on the consumer side by
`delivery/idempotency`. The real receiver keys a message by `(sender,
incarnation, sequence)` only, so the piece isolates one receiver history per
schedule instead of mapping schedules onto integer senders:
`consumer_dedup(histories, id, value)` looks up the history for
`id.schedule_id` (a map held by the consumer owner, empty history when absent),
builds `Message{sender = 0, incarnation = generation, sequence = scheduled
instant, value}`, runs the real `Cure.Elzoo.Idempotency.receiver/2`, stores
the next history under the schedule id and returns `{histories, :emit |
:suppress}`. Two schedules with equal generation and instant cannot collide
because their histories are different; within one schedule the second
presentation of an occurrence is suppressed and a redefinition's generation is
a new incarnation. `forget_schedule(histories, schedule_id)` retires a history
when the schedule is retired; after it, a re-presented occurrence emits again,
which is the documented lifecycle, not a defect. Retention inside a history is
the idempotency Piece's own Contract (unbounded, volatile, discarded on
restart). The mapping needs nothing beyond the occurrence id, so there is no
"inability to establish the mapping" case; an invalid id is refused. `delivery/time` is the clock port in the
recipe's own sense: the owning shell reads the clock and schedules ticks with
`Process.send_after`, passes the instant and the boundary as time events into
the pure transition, and cancels the timer reference to stop. The test owner
follows that recipe with a controllable clock function in place of
`System.system_time/1`; the piece itself never reads a clock.

**Timezone and DST** are declared, not computed. Fixture instants are UTC
milliseconds; the test shows identity behaviour for supplied instants, not the
correctness of any timezone mapping.

Non-goals: cron parsing, transport, job execution, durable storage, webhook
and app-event triggers, cancellation of dispatched work.

## Pure model

```
type OverlapPolicy = Skip | Replace | Queue(Nat)          # Nat = Z | S(Nat)
rec Schedule      { schedule_id: String, generation: Int, policy: OverlapPolicy, payload: String }
rec OccurrenceId  { schedule_id: String, generation: Int, scheduled_unix_ms: Int }
rec Occurrence    { id: OccurrenceId, payload: String }
rec ScheduleState { schedule: Schedule, last_recorded_unix_ms: Int, unsettled: List(Occurrence) }
rec Arrival       { now_unix_ms: Int, available_since_unix_ms: Int }
type Origin = Live | CatchUp
type SlotDecision = Admitted(OccurrenceId, Origin) | AlreadyRecorded(Int) | NotDue
                  | SkippedOffline | SkippedOverlap | SkippedQueueFull(Origin) | Replaced(OccurrenceId, OccurrenceId)
rec SlotResult    { state: ScheduleState, decision: SlotDecision }
```

The production transition is `admit_slot(state, slot, arrival) -> SlotResult`,
a chain of total dispatch functions, each taking the Bool it needs as an
argument computed at the call site (`recorded = slot <= last`,
`not_due = now < slot`, `offline = slot < available_since`, `room(n, xs)`
structural on `Nat` and the list). Laws generalize each Bool to a variable,
prove both branches, and instantiate with the production expression, so each
law is about the production function by definitional equality. The Elixir
boundary calls only `admit_slot`, `settle`, `admit_delivery`, `redefine` and
`identify`; it never calls a step with its own decision.

## Selected laws (Cure, checked by the pinned compiler)

Quantification: every parameter is universally quantified; `sid`, `payload`
arbitrary strings, `gen`, `last`, `slot`, `now`, `available_since` arbitrary
`Int`, `xs` an arbitrary list of occurrences, `n` an arbitrary `Nat`, `a`,
`a1`, `a2` arbitrary arrivals. Admissible configurations are built by
reducible constructors `skip_state`, `replace_state`, `queue_state`. "Live
hypotheses" means `slot <= last ≡ False()`, `now_of(a) < slot ≡ False()`,
`slot < available_since_of(a) ≡ False()` for the arrival in question.

| Law | Statement | Hypotheses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `identify_schedule_id` | `schedule_id_of(identify(sid, gen, slot)) ≡ sid` | none |
| `identify_generation` | `generation_of(identify(sid, gen, slot)) ≡ gen` | none |
| `identify_instant` | `instant_of(identify(sid, gen, slot)) ≡ slot` | none |
| `live_identity_skip` | `unsettled(admit_slot(skip_state(sid, gen, payload, last, []), slot, a)) ≡ [Occurrence{identify(sid, gen, slot), payload}]` | live hypotheses for `a` |
| `live_identity_replace` | `unsettled(admit_slot(replace_state(sid, gen, payload, last, xs), slot, a)) ≡ [Occurrence{identify(sid, gen, slot), payload}]` | live hypotheses for `a` |
| `live_identity_queue` | `unsettled(admit_slot(queue_state(n, sid, gen, payload, last, xs), slot, a)) ≡ [Occurrence{identify(sid, gen, slot), payload} \| xs]` | live hypotheses for `a`, `room(n, xs) ≡ True()` |
| `arrival_independent_skip` | `unsettled(admit_slot(skip_state(..., []), slot, a1)) ≡ unsettled(admit_slot(skip_state(..., []), slot, a2))` | live hypotheses for `a1` and for `a2` |
| `arrival_independent_replace` | same shape for `replace_state(..., xs)` | same |
| `arrival_independent_queue` | same shape for `queue_state(n, ..., xs)` | same and `room(n, xs) ≡ True()` |
| `within_one_skip` | `Within(one, unsettled(admit_slot(skip_state(sid, gen, payload, last, xs), slot, a)))` | `Within(one, xs)` |
| `within_one_replace` | same for `replace_state` | `Within(one, xs)` |
| `within_bound_queue` | `Within(n, unsettled(admit_slot(queue_state(n, ...), slot, a)))` | `Within(n, xs)` |
| `recorded_keeps_state` | `state_of(admit_slot(state, slot, a)) ≡ state` | `slot <= last_of(state) ≡ True()` |
| `not_due_keeps_state` | `state_of(admit_slot(state, slot, a)) ≡ state` | `slot <= last_of(state) ≡ False()`, `now_of(a) < slot ≡ True()` |
| `offline_skip_keeps_unsettled` | `unsettled(admit_slot(skip_state(..., xs), slot, a)) ≡ xs` and `last_of(...) ≡ slot` | `slot <= last ≡ False()`, `now_of(a) < slot ≡ False()`, `slot < available_since_of(a) ≡ True()` |
| `offline_replace_keeps_unsettled` | same for `replace_state` | same |
| `stale_redefinition_keeps_state` | `state_of(redefine(state, schedule)) ≡ state` | `greater_generation(state, schedule) ≡ False()` |
| `redefinition_records_displaced` | `displaced(redefine(state, schedule)) ≡ ids(unsettled(state))` and `unsettled(state_of(redefine(...))) ≡ []` | `valid(schedule) ≡ True()`, `same_id(state, schedule) ≡ True()`, `greater_generation(state, schedule) ≡ True()` |
| `unknown_settlement_keeps_state` | `state_of(settle(state, id, kind)) ≡ state` | `find(id, unsettled(state)) ≡ None()` |
| `stale_settlement_keeps_state` | `state_of(settle(state, id, kind)) ≡ state` | `id.generation < generation_of(state) ≡ True()` |
| `absent_delivery_rejected` | `admitted(admit_delivery(state, id, payload)) ≡ False()` | `find(id, unsettled(state)) ≡ None()` |
| `mismatched_delivery_rejected` | `admitted(admit_delivery(state, id, payload)) ≡ False()` | `find(id, unsettled(state)) ≡ Some(found)`, `text_equal(payload_of(found), payload) ≡ False()` |

Supporting: `Within indices (n: Nat, xs: List(Occurrence))` with
`within_nil : Within(n, [])`, `within_cons : Within(n, t) -> Within(S(n), [h | t])`;
`room_true_within(n, xs, x, h: room(n, xs) ≡ True()) -> Within(n, [x | xs])`
by induction on `n` and `xs`; a Bool-generalized lemma per dispatch layer.
`admit_delivery` returns a decision only, so it changes no state by type.

What Cure checks: exactly these statements for all values of the quantified
parameters, under the named hypotheses. What it does not check: the truth of
the Bool comparisons for concrete integers (they are hypotheses; the Elixir
boundary and tests cover them), behaviour across several calls, durability,
concurrency, or the availability predicate.

## Type/contract check and negative controls

The pinned Cure compiler (`Cure.Compiler.check_source` with the project source
roots) checks the module; the test audits the environment (no axioms, holes,
unresolved, unaudited, opaque or not-proven-total items). Negative controls,
each compiled separately and required to fail at the named law with a
conversion failure:

- production `identify` mutated to `generation + 1`: `identify_generation`
  fails (and `live_identity_*` fail against the mutated expected value only if
  they are stated with the literal record; they are stated with `identify`, so
  the field law is the control for the identity computation itself);
- production `identify` mutated to a constant scheduled instant: `identify_instant` fails;
- production admitting branch mutated to build the occurrence from a different
  slot (`slot + 1`): `live_identity_*` fail;
- production queue placement mutated to admit at capacity (`room(Z, xs) = true`): `within_bound_queue` fails;
- production skip placement mutated to append when an occurrence is unsettled: `within_one_skip` fails;
- production watermark dispatch mutated so an already recorded slot is admitted: `recorded_keeps_state` fails;
- production redefinition mutated so an equal generation replaces pending work: `stale_redefinition_keeps_state` fails.

Each control is a replacement in the production body, verified by the test to
differ from the source. The Elixir boundary validates foreign terms and is
tested separately.

## Tests (named scenarios, finite evidence)

1. two activations of one slot: sequential replay (second call gives
   `already_recorded{slot}`) and two concurrent processes through one
   serialized test owner (a GenServer in the test file) give one occurrence
   and one `already_recorded`; conclusion scoped to serialized ownership;
2. restart with an unsettled occurrence: `restore/3` from watermark and
   unsettled ids; re-presented slot `already_recorded`; retry delivery
   admitted with the same id; settlement clears it; a later duplicate delivery
   `unknown_occurrence`;
3. replay after a skipped slot, after settlement and after a redefinition:
   each gives `already_recorded{slot}` with no occurrence id and no state change;
4. skip: overlap skipped, settled then next slot admitted;
5. queue(2): two admitted, third `queue_full`, settlement frees one, next admitted;
6. replace: `replaced{old, new}`, old delivery and old settlement rejected;
7. offline catch-up: slots before the boundary skipped under skip and replace
   (watermark advances), admitted under queue up to `n` with origin
   `offline_catch_up`, excess `queue_full`; a live slot after an offline slot
   in the same batch follows the overlap policy;
8. clock and ordering edges: slot equal to the boundary is live; slot equal to
   `now` is due; slot after `now` refuses the batch; `available_since > now`
   refuses; missing or non-integer boundary refuses; unsorted batch refuses;
   equal slots in one batch give one occurrence and one `already_recorded`;
   initial watermark excludes earlier slots;
9. generation: equal and lower generation rejected with pending work intact;
   greater generation records every displaced id and empties the set;
   settlement and delivery of displaced ids `stale_generation`; watermark
   kept across redefinition;
10. delivery: payload mismatch; unknown id; admitted delivery leaves state
    unchanged (same term);
11. identity: `identify/3` fields, `encode_id/1` and `decode_id/1` round trip
    on ids with separators in the schedule id; fall-back fixture (two
    instants, two ids), shifted spring fixture (one instant, one id);
12. configuration: invalid policy, bound outside 1..1024, empty id, negative
    generation, non-binary payload, malformed states refused;
13. composition (as written): a DeliveryResult built with
    `Elzoo.Envelope.encode_result/1`, validated by `validate_result/1`, is
    translated by `settlement_from_result/1` for each outcome row of the
    Envelope README table, and the acknowledging and releasing rows settle the
    occurrence while `timed_out` and `unknown` leave it unsettled; an invalid
    result map is refused; `consumer_dedup/3`: the same occurrence presented
    twice emits once; two different schedules with equal generation and
    instant each emit once (isolation, the collision scenario); a
    redefinition's new generation emits again as a new incarnation; after
    `forget_schedule/2` the occurrence emits again; the real
    `Cure.Elzoo.Idempotency.receiver/2` is the receiver in every case; the test owner drives one
    admission per `Process.send_after` tick with the instant read from a

14. seeded random trace over the three policies against an independent

15. Cure audit and the seven negative controls.

## New findings requiring resolution

1. **BLOCKING — idempotency key mapping can suppress a different occurrence.** `ELZ-027.md` composition maps an occurrence to `(sender, generation, scheduled instant)` and states only “a caller-assigned integer sender per schedule”. The real `delivery/idempotency` receiver identifies a message by exactly `(sender, incarnation, sequence)`, not schedule ID or value. Two different schedules assigned the same integer sender, with equal generation and due instant, become the same key; the second is suppressed, despite being a distinct occurrence. State and enforce a stable injective sender mapping across every schedule sharing receiver history, or isolate histories by schedule with explicit lifecycle/retention Needs. Specify behavior on a collision or inability to establish the mapping. Plan real-receiver tests for two distinct schedules at equal generation/slot emitting once each, duplicate suppression within each, generation changes, and the selected collision/isolation boundary. This is a translation obligation, not a demand to add durable idempotency.

## Blocking findings

1. **The specified link and monitor combination does not deliver the claimed DOWN.** In the pinned upstream [`exec.erl`](https://github.com/saleyn/erlexec/blob/2.6.0/src/exec.erl), `do_run` around line 1282 selects `link` whenever both options occur; `ospid_init` around lines 1341–1349 sets `IsMon` only for `monitor` or synchronous calls. Thus the proposed `exec:run` options in proposal line 173 choose a link and do not produce the provider's synthetic `{'DOWN', OsPid, ...}` assumed throughout T11–T20. A real BEAM monitor of the proxy is a possible design direction, but its reference, `:noproc`, termination-reason and message-order races need an explicit state machine and tests, not an assertion that both options do both jobs. The E6 linked-owner probe and E2/E3 monitor probes do not test their combination. T21 also lacks a handoff for owner death after dispatch but before `{ref, os_pid}` reaches the Adapter: the provider may eventually stop, but the Adapter has no specified PGID, output or reply semantics. T22 says the Adapter's own in-memory registry is rebuilt after that Adapter dies without identifying a surviving registry or reconstruction protocol. T20 explicitly has no runtime test, contrary to one test per row.

2. **The provider does not meet the claimed safety/grace mechanics merely because it owns the signal.** [`exec_impl.cpp`](https://github.com/saleyn/erlexec/blob/2.6.0/c_src/exec_impl.cpp) `stop_child` lines 1370–1390 tries SIGKILL immediately if the initial TERM call fails; it does not wait the configured grace or establish a live group on that path. `check_children` around lines 1518–1525 unconditionally sends group TERM after the leader's exit, and `check_child_exit` / `reap_pid` around lines 1661–1699, as well as `check_child`, reap via `waitpid`/`wait4` before placing the status in `exited_children`. The assertion in proposal lines 82–85 that the at-exit TERM is sent while the leader's PID is still reserved is therefore unsupported and, on this path, false. If no original group member remains, PGID reuse before the subsequent group signal is not mechanically excluded; the proposal itself lists the no-wrap assumption. The provider's documented or pinned behavior does not itself discharge the task's full-grace, group identity and unsafe-signaling requirements. E2–E4 are finite happy-path observations and do not force TERM failure, natural exit during grace, reuse or handoff races. Treat upstream behavior as an exposed limitation requiring a design that meets the existing contract, or a deliberately reviewed change to it; merely relocating signals is not resolution.

3. **An ending signal is not necessarily the observed highest escalation stage.** E2/E3 show the signal which ended the leader, not a complete trace of all provider signal requests or the highest group escalation. An already independently dying leader, request failure, concurrent stop/deadline, and the unconditional post-exit TERM can make observed leader status and actual group-level signaling diverge. The existing acceptance requires the escalation stage reached be reported from observation. A truthful unknown result is appropriate when not observable; A8 as drafted would make definitive `terminated` carry an unobserved stage, explicitly relaxing that requirement. A8 also asserts the provider *performed* a staged termination on owner death, but a post-death empty `pgrep` alone cannot establish the request occurred, especially if the leader exited concurrently or the link was not yet established. T21 is therefore insufficient even if A8 were approved.

4. **Bounded cleanup remains unsatisfied on recognized ordinary paths.** T13 allows an in-group TERM-ignoring descendant to survive with no owner and no bound after the leader exits, including when the leader exits during a deadline or owner-death stop. The issue explicitly requires timeout and collector death leave no surviving process group, and A6 explicitly says unknown does not relax the invariant or acceptance. A declared residual is not an implementation of that requirement. T20 calls the provider a bounded cleanup owner even when the reason is a stuck provider, without evidence its watchdog can make progress. E16 directly exhibits an orphan on port-program SIGKILL; E12 is application stop in one setup, not proof of every VM-death/finalize case. `finalize()` also uses an alarm, so an unconditional bound regardless of failures or grace configuration needs substantiation. T25 has no `members` observation in its stated return although the resolution claims members are reported in both unbounded cases. A signal-success-without-delivery control from G6 is absent from the T1–T30 test plan.

5. **Exec/dispatch outcome is still false in an explicitly known case.** E11b demonstrates `execve` failure and a real exit 1 are indistinguishable; a `File.stat` validation cannot establish the later exec succeeded (replacement, permissions, loader or interpreter errors). Proposal lines 247–249 and D3 admit reporting an undispatched command as `completed/dispatched`, contrary to the issue's result mapping and A1. Merely asking to accept the label without a precise reviewed contract change does not make the proposed contract consistent. T10 must also prove that every `exec:run` error means no command dispatched, rather than infer this from a bad-option test.
